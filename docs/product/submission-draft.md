# Before the First Fire on the Moon
Team working copy for the English project form.

**Summary.** Flight Dynamics helps lunar habitat engineers find the closest published flammability tests for a material and atmosphere. In one set of short centrifuge tests, Mylar film burned at 15.6% oxygen under lunar-equivalent gravity, below the reported microgravity and Earth limits. An engineer can trace those observations to the paper, examine differences in experimental conditions, and see when a proposed cabin atmosphere falls outside the evidence reviewed.

**How it works.** Ask for a material and specify oxygen, airflow and pressure. The service ranks source records by similarity to lunar conditions and returns a matching test, related observations or an evidence gap. PSI coverage compares solid-fuel flight and ground experiments. A separate gravity curve displays measured burn and extinction limits. Three dated team predictions specify what future FM2 observations could confirm or refute. The optional 3D chamber illustrates flow and heat transport using a generic fuel source.

**NASA data and references.** NASA Physical Sciences Informatics supplies experimental tables for [BASS-II](https://doi.org/10.60555/4qc4-de67), [BASS](https://doi.org/10.60555/58h4-dy80), [Saffire-I](https://doi.org/10.60555/0t15-1z43), [Saffire-II](https://doi.org/10.60555/2chp-m469) and [Saffire-III](https://doi.org/10.60555/040q-4h36). We use 159 curated PSI table rows. The lunar comparison comes from [Olson & Ferkul, Table I, p. 6](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20130010991). Other sources include [Urban et al. 2025](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20250002114), the [BASS-II report](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20210011385), [LUCI](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20250010653) and the [FM2 poster](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20240015307). Each result links to its source and location.

**Use of AI.** The team used Claude for research and evidence extraction and Codex for implementation, testing and the two cached example explanations. The app ranks records in code. With server-side Anthropic credentials, a model extracts explicit query fields and drafts an explanation from the retrieved records. Code checks cited IDs and numbers, requests one correction for invalid output, and can fall back to a verified template. The public demo also supports saved examples. Report live provider operation in the final form only after testing it with the production credentials.

**Validation and limits.** A comparison against the published Table I checked all 12 material/gravity rows and all 24 oxygen-limit values. This checks transcription for that table only. In the short reduced-gravity experiments, the criterion was burning through a 5.18-second drop. The Earth test used a 15.2 cm burn-length criterion, and microgravity used 30 cm/s forced ventilation. The three FM2 predictions are the team's inferences. Missing information remains visible.
