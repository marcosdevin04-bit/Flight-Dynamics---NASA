How it works

Why gravity matters for fire

  1. 1

    Tested on Earth

    NASA's standard flame test runs in Earth gravity, where rising hot gas controls how a flame gets oxygen and loses heat.

  2. 2

    Different on the Moon

    At one-sixth gravity that flow is much weaker. Depending on the material and the cabin pressure, a flame can survive at lower oxygen, or need more.

  3. 3

    Checked by FM2

    NASA's first fire experiment on the Moon will settle it. We wrote down what we expect before it flies, so anyone can check us.

Why this matters to a team in Uruguay

On February 25, 2012, a fire in the machinery room of Brazil’s Comandante Ferraz station spread through the Antarctic base. Uruguay’s Artigas station stands on the same island. For our team in Montevideo, that puts a human question behind the data: when help is far away, what can an engineer learn about a material before choosing it for a habitat?

Antarctica gives us the question. To study how gravity changes burning, we turn to NASA’s experiments. Brazilian Navy account ↗ · Uruguay’s Antarctic stations ↗
